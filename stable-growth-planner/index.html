Where to invest: instruments to evaluate

FDIC-insured high-yield savings / CDs

Best for capital certainty and near-term spending. Compare APY, deposit-insurance limits, CD penalties and maturity dates.

US Treasury bills and notes

Held to maturity, they offer predictable nominal principal and coupon payments, backed by the US government. Treasury interest is generally exempt from state/local income taxes.

Short-term Treasury ETF (e.g., SGOV, VGSH)

Convenient and liquid, but ETF prices and future distributions can change; unlike individual Treasuries held to maturity, no fixed payout is promised.

Investment-grade bond ETF (e.g., BSV, SCHZ)

May offer income and diversification, but carries rate and credit risk. SCHZ has broader duration exposure than a short-term Treasury holding.

Broad stock ETF (e.g., VTI, SCHB)

Growth potential over 10 years, but can decline sharply and is unsuitable for money you cannot afford to lose at your spending date.

Protect capital: before, during and after a crash

BEFORE

Separate emergency savings from investable money. Ladder insured CDs or individual Treasuries to the years you need cash. Keep equity exposure small when the spending date is fixed.

DURING

Avoid forced sales. Let short-maturity instruments mature; do not assume bond ETFs or stocks cannot fall. Check your allocation instead of reacting to headlines.

AFTER

Rebalance to the original risk target only if your spending timeline and capacity for loss are unchanged. As your spending date approaches, move planned withdrawals to cash and matched maturities.

Buffett-inspired: avoid unnecessary complexity and permanent capital loss. Dalio-inspired: diversify across risks and stress-test scenarios. Neither approach guarantees positive returns.

Inflation stays elevated

Consider TIPS (VTIP) and Treasury maturities aligned to spending dates; inflation protection is imperfect over short periods.

AI stocks correct sharply

Limit sector bets; broad funds still have mega-cap technology exposure. Short Treasuries and cash reduce equity sensitivity.

Layoffs / recession

Keep a separate emergency fund. Avoid forced equity sales; Treasury ladders and insured deposits support planned withdrawals.

Risk and tax notes: ETFs and bond funds are not FDIC insured, can lose value, and do not lock in a maturity payout. Individual Treasury securities held to maturity offer contractual nominal payments backed by the US government. CDs are insured only within applicable limits and conditions. Stocks and sector ETFs can decline significantly; single-company shares are not appropriate as a core stable-return allocation. In taxable accounts, Treasury interest is generally exempt from state/local income tax, while fund distribution tax treatment varies.
